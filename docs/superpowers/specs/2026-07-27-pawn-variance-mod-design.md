# Pawn Variance Mod — Design

## Overview

A RimWorld mod that adds configurable, randomized variance to a pawn's skill levels, trait count/quality, and passion assignment at generation time. Each pawn rolls one hidden continuous "quality" value (0-1, Beta-distributed) that smoothly biases all three systems in the same probabilistic direction, without ever guaranteeing that a high-quality roll produces the best possible outcome across all three. There are no discrete tiers in the mechanism — "Incompetent/Standard/Specialist/Prodigy" exist only as computed display labels for a given quality value, never as boundaries that change behavior. Each system also has its own on/off toggle, defaulting on, so users can keep vanilla behavior for any subset of the three, plus its own independent "noise" control governing how loosely that system tracks the shared quality value.

## Goals

- Let users control the distribution via a small, direct settings surface: one "average pawn quality" slider plus one independent "noise" slider per system (skill/trait/passion) — no per-tier fields, no hidden brackets.
- Loosely correlate skill/trait/passion outcomes per pawn without hard-coupling them — a high-quality roll should *usually* skew good everywhere but must retain a real chance of a mediocre or bad outcome in any individual system, and there must be no discontinuity ("seam") between two pawns whose underlying quality rolls are nearly identical.
- Work safely with mods that add/change traits and skills, without hand-maintained knowledge of specific mods' content.
- Be safe to add or remove mid-save with zero save-file corruption risk.
- Default to applying to all humanlike pawns (colonists, prisoners, wanderers, quest pawns, raiders/hostiles) with a toggle to exclude hostile-faction pawns.
- Let users see at a glance, in a pawn's own bio, an approximate color-coded tier readout (Incompetent/Standard/Specialist/Prodigy) — cosmetic only, never affects mechanics.

## Non-goals

- No automated unit test harness (impractical for a Harmony-patched RimWorld mod; verification is manual/in-game, see Testing).
- No custom UI beyond the standard vanilla Mod Settings window (including a small live tier-readout label drawn inside that window next to the average-quality slider — it's still the standard settings window, not a new dialog/panel) and the one bio-text tier label described below (no new dialogs/panels/inspect-string patches beyond that single append).
- No multi-RimWorld-version support (targets current stable 1.5/1.6 only).
- No retroactive re-rolling of a pawn who has already received (or was already eligible to receive) their one-time quality roll — i.e. any pawn that was Adult-stage, or non-Biotech, at the point this mod was added or a toggle was off stays permanently as-is. This does **not** cover a Biotech pawn who was still Baby/Child-stage (and thus had no roll yet, eligible or not) at that point: such a pawn still gets exactly one quality roll and one variance application, at the first moment they're actually eligible — either at generation if already Adult, or at the Child→Adult growth transition otherwise (see Core Algorithms > Growth-up variance). This is a single first-and-only roll per pawn, not a re-roll of one that already happened.
- No new TraitDefs, HediffDefs, or other content Defs — the mod only reweights selection among already-loaded Defs (vanilla + whatever mods are active).

## Architecture

Standard Harmony-patched RimWorld mod, two patch points:

- `PawnVarianceMod : Mod` — entry point; loads settings, draws the Mod Settings window.
- `PawnVarianceSettings : ModSettings` — all configurable values; `ExposeData` for persistence in mod config (not the save file).
- `HarmonyPatches` — one postfix on `PawnGenerator.GeneratePawn`.
- `GrowthUpPatch` — a second, small Harmony postfix on Biotech's Child→Adult developmental-stage transition method (exact target method flagged for implementation-time verification, same caveat as `GeneratePawn`'s call sites below). Handles the case in Per-pawn flow step 2 where a pawn was skipped at generation for being Baby/Child-stage: the moment such a pawn crosses into Adult, this patch re-applies the same hostile-pawn/toggle gating as generation-time, rolls that pawn's one-and-only `quality` value, and runs the three appliers, so a home-grown or adopted child born/received while still developing isn't permanently vanilla just because the initial generation postfix declined to touch them. Guarded by an in-memory (non-`Scribe`-persisted) processed-pawn set so a pawn can't be double-varied if the target method ever fires more than once for the same pawn (see Core Algorithms > Growth-up variance for why this matters more here than the analogous `GeneratePawn` retry case). Gated by its own setting, `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` (see Settings Schema) — independent of the three *system* toggles, since a user may want variance for pawns generated as adults but prefer developing pawns to simply stay vanilla for life; this independence does not extend to `applyToHostilePawns` or the system toggles themselves, both of which growth-up still honors. This is distinct from `TierLabelPatch` below, which is purely cosmetic and never mutates pawn stats (though, as detailed on that bullet, it isn't gating-free either).
- `QualityRoller` — rolls one continuous `quality ∈ [0,1]` per pawn from a Beta distribution parameterized by the "average pawn quality" slider and a fixed internal population-spread constant (recomputed only when the mean slider changes, not per pawn).
- `SkillVarianceApplier` — for each skill, computes a smooth quality-derived baseline shift plus an independent per-skill noise offset (scaled by the skill noise slider), additive on top of vanilla-generated levels, clamped to [0, 20].
- `TraitVarianceApplier` — computes trait count as a smooth function of quality; selects traits via continuous weighted sampling against a quality-derived target desirability score, with the sampling `spread` parameter widened/narrowed by the trait noise slider; respects vanilla trait conflict/exclusion checks.
- `PassionVarianceApplier` — computes passion count as a smooth function of quality; places passions weighted by each skill's *final absolute level* (post-variance if skills enabled, vanilla level if not, so it has no dependency on the skill toggle's state), with weighting "peakedness" controlled by the passion noise slider. **Known, accepted scope limit**: passion *count* always tracks `quality` regardless of the skill toggle, but passion *placement* (which skills get them) is only correlated with `quality` when skill variance is enabled, since it's vanilla's own uncorrelated skill levels that get used otherwise. No passion-noise-slider setting changes this — noise controls placement *peakedness* given whatever levels are being weighted, not what those levels are correlated to. This is deliberate (per-system toggles must stay decoupled, per Goals) rather than a bug, but it means "skill variance off, passion variance on" is a real, intentional exception to the general "noise sliders control correlation strength" claim below.
- `TraitDesirabilityCache` — `[StaticConstructorOnStartup]` static cache: for every loaded `TraitDef`+degree, computes a heuristic desirability score once from its actual effects (skill offsets, stat offsets/factors, work-tag disables, social effects) — no hand-curated per-trait table, works identically for vanilla and modded traits, requires zero maintenance as trait mods are added/updated/removed.
- `TierLabelPatch` — separate, small Harmony postfix appending a color-coded tier label to the pawn's bio/description text (see "Tier bio label" below). Deliberately a second, independent patch rather than folded into `HarmonyPatches`' `GeneratePawn` postfix, since it fires on a different method at a different time (description text is generated on-demand when the character info panel is opened, not at pawn-generation time) and has nothing to do with mutating pawn stats. Independent of *timing*, not of *eligibility*: it re-checks the same gates the main postfix uses (humanlike, `applyToHostilePawns`, all-toggles-off, and — if Biotech is active — `DevelopmentalStage == Adult`) against the pawn's *current* state before appending anything, and appends nothing at all if any gate fails. Without this, a pawn the mod deliberately left 100% vanilla (an excluded hostile, a pawn generated while every toggle was off, a still-developing Biotech child) would get a fabricated tier readout implying they received a quality roll they never got.

### Per-pawn flow

1. Vanilla `GeneratePawn` completes fully (all vanilla skills/traits/passions generated normally, **including gear/apparel/weapon selection and health-condition generation, which read the pawn's original, pre-variance traits and skill levels** — since this mod's postfix runs only after `GeneratePawn` returns, any trait it adds/removes or skill level it shifts cannot retroactively influence choices vanilla already made using the un-varied pawn. E.g. a pawn who gains `Trigger-happy` or a shifted-up Shooting level from this mod's postfix was still equipped as if they didn't have it. **Accepted, not fixed**: reaching into vanilla's gear/health generation to re-run it against post-variance stats would mean patching deep into pipeline internals well beyond this mod's two stated patch points, for a cosmetic-adjacent mismatch (equipment/health quality trailing the "true" post-variance pawn slightly) rather than a correctness or save-safety problem — consistent with this mod's scope being skill/trait/passion values themselves, not everything vanilla derives from them at generation time). **Unverified assumption, flagged for implementation-time confirmation** (requires checking the target RimWorld version's decompiled `PawnGenerator`, not just this spec): (a) `GeneratePawn` is known historically to internally retry/regenerate a candidate pawn multiple times before returning a single validated result — if still true, the postfix (and quality roll/RNG draws) may fire more than once per logical pawn, which only matters for RNG-draw-count bookkeeping, not correctness, since discarded candidates are simply discarded; (b) whether quest-reward/trader-redress pawn flows route back through this same method or a separate path that skips it is unconfirmed — if the latter, some of the pawn categories Goals promises variance for (quest pawns, traders) could silently bypass the patch and need a second patch point. **This is not merely a cosmetic implementation detail like the other flagged verifications** — Goals bullet 5 explicitly promises variance for quest pawns, so if (b) turns out true this spec has an open commitment with no named fallback. Resolve at implementation start, before writing `HarmonyPatches`: decompile the target version's quest-reward and trader-redress pawn-generation call paths; if either bypasses `GeneratePawn`, add a second, narrowly-scoped postfix on whatever method it does call, reusing `QualityRoller` and the three appliers unchanged (only the entry-point patch differs).
2. Postfix fires. Early-exit immediately if: pawn is not `RaceProps.Humanlike`, or all three toggles are off, or (`applyToHostilePawns` is off and `pawn.Faction?.HostileTo(Faction.OfPlayer) == true`), or (Biotech is active and `pawn.DevelopmentalStage != DevelopmentalStage.Adult`). The developmental-stage check itself needs no per-source special-casing, since it's a property read directly off the `Pawn` at postfix time, not something derived from how it was generated — it applies uniformly to whichever pawns *do* reach this postfix regardless of origin (birth, quest reward, adoption, wanderer join, purchase). Whether *every* such origin actually reaches this postfix at all is the separate, still-unverified question flagged in step 1 above (quest/trader flows potentially routing around `GeneratePawn` entirely) — that uncertainty is unresolved by anything here and applies equally to Baby/Child pawns as to Adults. Vanilla deliberately withholds personality traits and meaningful skill/passion state from Baby/Child-stage pawns, building them up gradually through growth moments instead, so this mod must not overwrite that with a full adult-style roll. See "Growth-up variance" below for what happens to these pawns later.
3. `QualityRoller` rolls one continuous `quality` value for this pawn.
4. Each enabled applier (skill/trait/passion) runs independently *in the sense that matters for the Goals* — each rolls its own noise off the shared `quality` value, no system's noise slider or toggle state controls another's, and no hard boundary exists anywhere in the mapping from quality to output. This does **not** mean the appliers are order-independent or side-effect-free with respect to each other: `PassionVarianceApplier` deliberately reads `SkillVarianceApplier`'s final output (see Passion variance step 3), so `SkillVarianceApplier` must run first whenever both are enabled, in the same postfix invocation, before `PassionVarianceApplier` builds its weighted skill list. There is a second, independent reason passion must run last: `TraitVarianceApplier` can add or remove traits that carry `disabledWorkTags`, which changes which skills are `SkillRecord.TotallyDisabled` — and Passion variance step 3 explicitly excludes totally-disabled skills from placement. So the required order, whenever more than one is enabled, is **trait, then skill, then passion** (trait and skill have no dependency on each other and could run in either order relative to one another, but both must precede passion). These are intentional one-way data dependencies (skill result → passion input; trait result → passion's disabled-skill filter), not a violation of "independent" — nothing about passion variance ever feeds back into skill or trait variance, and passion's *count* still tracks `quality` directly regardless of this ordering.
5. Entire postfix body wrapped in try/catch; any exception is logged and vanilla generation is left untouched (fail-safe to vanilla, never to a broken/partial state). Logging uses `Log.ErrorOnce` keyed by exception type+stack trace (not a single global "log the very first failure ever" flag) — a *systematic* bug (e.g. a null-ref triggered by one specific modded trait) must keep surfacing distinctly rather than silently going quiet after pawn #1, which would otherwise let the Testing Plan's manual distribution check pass by misreading "no variance happening" as "noise slider is just high." A `verboseLogging` dev-mode setting (default off) additionally rethrows instead of catching, for use during manual testing.

## Core Algorithms

### Quality roll
Roll one `quality ∈ [0,1]` per pawn from a Beta distribution. The Beta distribution's shape parameters are derived from the "average pawn quality" slider (target mean `m`) and a fixed internal concentration constant `k` (not user-exposed — see Settings Schema for rationale) via the standard mean-parameterization `α = m·k`, `β = (1-m)·k`. Before this derivation, `m` is clamped to `[ε, 1-ε]` for a small fixed `ε` (e.g. `1e-3`), **even though the slider's own valid range is the full closed `[0,1]`** — at `m` exactly 0 or 1 the formula above yields `α = 0` or `β = 0`, an invalid (undefined) Gamma shape parameter, not merely an extreme one. Testing Plan item 5 explicitly requires the slider to sit at exactly 0 and 1 without crashing, so this clamp is load-bearing, not defensive boilerplate. Recomputing `α`/`β` happens only when the mean slider changes, not per pawn. Beta is chosen over a clamped normal distribution specifically because it's naturally bounded to [0,1] with no clamping artifacts distorting the tails.

**Sampling implementation note**: there is no built-in Beta sampler in .NET or RimWorld's `Rand`. Standard construction: `Beta(α,β) = X/(X+Y)` where `X~Gamma(α,1)`, `Y~Gamma(β,1)`, using the Marsaglia-Tsang method for Gamma sampling (shape ≥ 1) with the standard shape-augmentation boost trick (sample at shape+1 via Marsaglia-Tsang, then multiply by `U^(1/shape)` — a consequence of Stuart's theorem, sometimes described as an addendum to Marsaglia-Tsang; **not** the Ahrens-Dieter algorithm, a distinct rejection-based method — worth naming correctly so an implementer researches the right technique) for the shape < 1 case — which will occur routinely here, since an extreme average-quality slider position combined with the fixed population-spread constant can push `α` or `β` below 1. This is a small, well-documented numerical routine, not a one-line `Rand` call — flagging explicitly so it isn't underestimated during implementation.

**Numerical floor on the shape parameters themselves**: the `ε` clamp above prevents `α`/`β` from being exactly 0, but very small shape values (well below 1) still stress the boost step: `U^(1/shape)` for `U ∈ (0,1)` and large `1/shape` can underflow to exactly `0.0` in double precision, which would make `X` or `Y` exactly 0 and, in the (rare but real) case both underflow in the same draw, produce `0/0 = NaN` for `quality`. Guard by (a) resampling if both `X` and `Y` land at exactly 0, and (b) treating an underflowed single-sided 0 as a legitimate extreme-but-valid draw (`quality → 0` or `1`) rather than an error. This only matters at the far ends of the "average pawn quality" slider, which is exactly what Testing Plan item 5 exercises.

**Expected consequence of a fixed concentration constant**: with `α = m·k`, `β = (1-m)·k` for fixed `k`, `Var(quality) ≈ m(1-m)/(k+1)`, which shrinks toward 0 as the mean slider `m` approaches 0 or 1. This is not a bug to fix — it's mathematically unavoidable for *any* distribution bounded to [0,1] (there's simply less room to spread out near a boundary) — but it is a real, user-visible consequence worth documenting explicitly: pushing "average pawn quality" to an extreme setting narrows the population's spread along with shifting its mean, so pawns become *more* uniform (uniformly excellent, or uniformly poor) rather than staying as spread-out as at `m = 0.5`. Testing Plan item 5 exercises exactly this setting and should watch for it as expected behavior, not just check for crashes.

There is no tier lookup anywhere — `quality` flows directly into the three appliers below as a continuous number.

### Trait desirability scoring (`TraitDesirabilityCache`)
For each loaded `TraitDef`+degree, compute a single scalar score by summing independently-normalized contributions from each effect category, so incommensurable units (skill points, stat percentages, boolean work-tag disables, social multipliers) don't get combined as raw numbers:
1. **Skill offsets**: sum the trait's skill-level offsets, divide by a fixed reference magnitude (e.g. ±6, RimWorld's largest vanilla skill offset), then **clamp the category's total to [-1, 1]**. (An earlier draft of this spec tried to fix multi-skill dominance by normalizing each skill's offset individually before summing rather than dividing once after the sum — that doesn't actually change anything: dividing every term by the same fixed constant and summing is algebraically identical whether the division happens before or after the sum, `Σ(xᵢ/c) = (Σxᵢ)/c`. The clamp is the part that actually bounds the category, not the ordering.) Clamping is what stops a trait touching many skills at once from blowing past the intended [-1, 1] contribution range and dominating the other three categories.
2. **Stat offsets/factors**: for each affected stat, convert a factor to a percent-deviation-from-1.0 (`factor - 1`) and treat offsets as already percent-like; sum, divide by a fixed reference magnitude (e.g. ±1.0), then clamp the category's total to [-1, 1] — same reasoning and same fix as category 1, for the same underlying issue (a trait affecting many stats shouldn't get an outsized combined contribution just from touching more of them).
3. **Work-tag disables**: a small fixed negative penalty per disabled work tag (disabling capability is close to universally negative for a colonist), summed and clamped to [-1, 1] the same way — a trait disabling many work tags at once shouldn't dominate purely by count.
4. **Social effects**: normalize opinion/social-fight-chance modifiers using the same divide-then-clamp *structure* as category 2, but **not the same reference magnitude** — category 2's ±1.0 is calibrated for stat *factors* converted to percent-deviation (`factor - 1`), which are typically small (e.g. 0.2 for a 20% boost), whereas opinion modifiers are raw point offsets on RimWorld's roughly ±100-point opinion scale and routinely run ±5 to ±20 or more. Reusing ±1.0 for those would divide a typical ±10 opinion offset by 1.0 and clamp virtually every socially-relevant trait to exactly +1 or -1, collapsing this category into a near-binary "has positive/negative social effect" flag instead of a meaningful continuous contribution — the same class of unit mismatch the tier-label formula's skill-level normalization had before its own fix (see Tier bio label, further below). Use a separate fixed reference magnitude sized for opinion-point scale instead (e.g. ±20), summed and clamped to [-1, 1] the same way as the other categories.
Sum the four normalized contributions (unweighted sum — no category is treated as inherently more important than another, since a modded trait mix could concentrate its whole effect in just one category and still needs a meaningful score). This produces an unbounded-but-typically-small real number per trait; `observedMinScore`/`observedMaxScore` (Settings Schema) are computed as `meanScore ± zMultiplier·stdDevScore` (clamped to the actual observed min/max, so the bounds never extend past a score that doesn't exist) across all currently-loaded traits, so absolute scale doesn't matter — only the relative ordering and spread across whatever's loaded.

**Known scope limitation — the four categories miss mood/thought and hardcoded-behavior effects entirely**: a meaningful share of vanilla traits express their real character almost entirely outside these four numeric-field categories — e.g. `Bloodlust` (berserk/execution behavior, no stat offsets), `Kind`/`Abrasive` (opinion given/received via hardcoded social-interaction logic, not a general stat field), `Nudist`/`Ascetic`/`Greedy`/`Jealous` (mood via room/apparel-quality-linked `ThoughtDef`s, not `TraitDegreeData` offsets), `Iron-willed`/`Volatile`/`Nervous` (hardcoded mental-break/behavior modifiers). None of these route through skill offsets, stat offsets/factors, work-tag disables, or the social fields scored above, so under this heuristic they score at or near 0 — indistinguishable from a genuinely neutral trait, even though many are clearly good or bad to a player. Accepted as a real gap in the heuristic rather than fixed: generically modeling arbitrary hardcoded behavior and linked-thought mood effects for any vanilla-or-modded `TraitDef` would require far more than a `[StaticConstructorOnStartup]` numeric-field scan, and defeats the "zero hand-maintained per-trait knowledge" design this cache exists for. In practice this means some traits will be weighted as neutral when they're not, which softens (but doesn't structurally break) the trait-variance goal for that specific subset.

**Why a mean/stddev bound, not a percentile or true min/max**: a true min/max lets `target` at `quality` near 0 or 1 sit exactly at whichever one or two outlier traits happen to define that extreme — the Gaussian weighting then concentrates almost entirely on that tiny cluster regardless of the noise slider, producing near-identical "prodigy"/"incompetent" trait sets and undercutting the Goals promise of real variety even at high quality. An order-statistic percentile (e.g. "2nd/98th percentile by rank") was considered and rejected: with a modest loaded trait pool — vanilla-only, or vanilla plus one or two light mods, plausibly well under 100 `TraitDef`+degree entries — trimming by rank barely moves the bound off the 1st/2nd-most-extreme entry, so it degenerates right back into the true-min/max outlier-anchoring problem it was meant to fix. A mean/stddev bound doesn't have this failure mode: it scales with the actual spread of whatever's loaded regardless of pool size, so it stays meaningful whether 40 traits are loaded or 4,000. `zMultiplier` (e.g. 2) is a fixed internal constant, not user-exposed, same tier as the other named constants in Settings Schema.

### Skill variance (if enabled)
For each skill independently:
1. Compute a smooth baseline shift: `baseline = lerp(globalMinShift, globalMaxShift, quality)` across the mod's configured global shift range, default **-6 to +6** (symmetric, so the default "average pawn quality" slider position of 0.5 yields `baseline = 0` — parity with vanilla, not a systematic boost). An earlier draft defaulted this to -4/+8, which put the default quality slider at `lerp(-4,8,0.5) = +2` — a silent +2 average skill boost over vanilla at the supposedly-neutral default setting, inconsistent with how the Trait count range and Passion count range defaults were both chosen so their quality=0.5 midpoint roughly matches vanilla's typical output (`lerp(1,6,0.5)=3.5` ≈ vanilla's ~3 traits; `lerp(0,3,0.5)=1.5` ≈ vanilla's ~1-2 passions). The symmetric -6/+6 range keeps the same total span (12) as before, just recentered on parity.
2. Add an independent per-skill noise offset, rolled from a triangular distribution (average of two uniform rolls, so it clusters near 0 rather than being flat — same "gradual, not boxed" rationale as the rest of the design) over `[-magnitude, +magnitude]`, where `magnitude = lerp(minMagnitudeFloor, maxMagnitude, skillNoiseSlider)` with `minMagnitudeFloor` a small nonzero constant (not exactly 0, for the same reason trait `spread` and passion `temperature` have nonzero floors — see below).
3. Add the result to the vanilla-generated level, clamp to [0, 20].

Store final per-skill levels for use by passion placement. No tier brackets exist, so there is no discontinuity between any two quality values, however close.

### Trait variance (if enabled)
1. Compute trait count as a smooth function of quality (e.g. `round(lerp(minCount, maxCount, quality) + smallRandomJitter)`), clamped to the user-configured Trait count range itself (Settings Schema) — i.e. jitter can shift the rounded count within/around that range but never push the final clamped count outside `[minCount, maxCount]`, the same explicit-bounds treatment Skill variance step 3 gives its own clamp to [0, 20].
2. Before clearing anything, capture the set of **forced/required traits** that must survive regardless of variance: `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits`, `PawnKindDef.forcedTraits` (**unverified, flagged for implementation-time confirmation** against the target version's decompiled source, same caveat as `GeneratePawn`'s call sites above — RimWorld's forced-trait mechanics for kind-defs/xenotypes may route through a differently-named field or through genes exclusively rather than a dedicated `PawnKindDef` list), and any gene-forced trait (Biotech genes that grant a specific `TraitDef`, e.g. via a gene's forced-trait hediff/ability). Also capture the set of **disallowed traits** from the same sources plus active Ideology precepts that prohibit specific traits for the pawn's ideoligion. These are not subject to variance — they exist for quest scripting, kind-def identity, xenotype identity, or ideo compatibility, and silently overwriting or banning them would break systems outside this mod's scope.

   **Known scope limitation — forced-trait detection is a closed, hand-enumerated list, in tension with the Goals bullet on mod-agnostic behavior**: unlike `TraitDesirabilityCache`, which scores *any* loaded `TraitDef` generically with zero per-mod knowledge, this forced-trait *protection* step only recognizes traits forced through the three vanilla/Biotech mechanisms named above. A mod that forces a trait through its own custom mechanism (a bespoke quest system, a custom heritage/race system, a non-Biotech "grants a trait" hediff) is invisible here — that trait isn't in the captured forced set, so step 3's clear-and-rebuild would wipe it like any other vanilla-random trait. Accepted rather than fixed: there's no generic, reflection-safe way to ask an arbitrary mod "did you just force this specific trait for a scripted reason," the way `TraitDesirabilityCache` can generically score a `TraitDef`'s numeric fields — protecting against this would require per-mod integration, exactly the "hand-maintained knowledge of specific mods' content" the mod-safety goal disclaims. In practice this only affects mods whose forced-trait mechanism doesn't route through `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits`, `PawnKindDef.forcedTraits`, or Biotech genes — most compatible content mods that need a guaranteed trait do route through one of those three, since they're the vanilla-supported mechanisms other mods are expected to build on.
3. Clear vanilla-generated traits *except* the forced set captured in step 2; re-add the forced set unconditionally (they don't count against the noise/weighting and are exempt from conflict-checking against each other, since vanilla itself guarantees they're mutually compatible). If the forced set alone already meets or exceeds the target count from step 1, the weighted-sampling step below simply has zero slots to fill — see the "Accepted limitation" note under Growth-up variance, which covers this generation-time case too, not just growth-up's.
4. Compute a quality-derived target desirability score: `target = lerp(observedMinScore, observedMaxScore, quality)`, where the observed bounds come from `TraitDesirabilityCache`'s startup scan of currently-loaded traits (see Settings Schema) — not a fixed constant.
5. Repeatedly sample from all eligible loaded traits (excluding the disallowed set from step 2) using continuous weighted sampling, filling only the remaining slots after the forced traits are placed: for each candidate compute `distSq = (score - target)²`, let `minDistSq` be the smallest `distSq` among all eligible candidates this pawn round, and set `weight = exp(-(distSq - minDistSq) / spread)`, where `spread = lerp(minSpreadFloor, maxSpread, traitNoiseSlider)` with `minSpreadFloor > 0` (never exactly 0 — see Trait/Passion noise-floor setting below; a zero spread would produce a NaN/degenerate weight array and collapse to a hard argmax cutoff, which would violate the "no hard cutoffs" pillar). Subtracting `minDistSq` before dividing (the same log-sum-exp stabilization used in Passion variance step 4) is load-bearing: without it, a tight `spread` combined with no candidate sitting near `target` can underflow every single weight to exactly `0.0`, leaving an all-zero weight array with nothing to sample (`0/0` on normalize) — a real failure mode distinct from, and not covered by, the "ran out of eligible candidates" case in step 6. Shifting by `minDistSq` guarantees the closest candidate always has weight exactly `1` and everything else is a finite value in `(0, 1]`, without changing the relative distribution. Only the **mod-list-dependent** parts are cached: the sorted list of `(TraitDef, score)` pairs and `observedMinScore`/`observedMaxScore`, rebuilt when the loaded mod list changes. `target`, `minDistSq`, and the resulting weight vector/cumulative sum are **per-pawn** (since `target` depends on that pawn's own `quality` roll) and are recomputed fresh for each pawn — this is a cheap O(number of loaded traits) pass, not something that can be precomputed once.
6. Skip any candidate that conflicts with already-picked traits (including the forced set from step 2) **or that the pawn already has** — sampling is without replacement, so each pick is removed from (or checked against, via `TraitSet.HasTrait`) the eligible pool before the next draw; a `TraitDef` can't be selected twice for the same pawn. This is reimplemented against `TraitDef`'s public `conflictingTraits`/`exclusionTags` fields and `TraitSet.HasTrait` — **field names unverified, flagged for implementation-time confirmation** against the target version's decompiled source, the same caveat already given to `PawnKindDef.forcedTraits` and vanilla's Minor/Major passion-ratio method elsewhere in this spec — mirroring vanilla's structural conflict rules directly rather than reflecting into vanilla's private generator method (which isn't a stable public API to depend on). If eligible candidates run out before the rolled count is filled, stop early with a `Log.Message` (expected with small trait pools, not an error).

This weighting is deliberately probabilistic, not a hard cutoff — a high-quality pawn can still roll a mediocre or bad trait, just less often than a low-quality pawn would, and the trait noise slider lets users directly control how often that happens. The per-pawn weight-array cost scales with the number of currently-loaded traits (low hundreds to low thousands of `TraitDef`s even with large trait-expansion modlists) — expected to stay cheap relative to the rest of pawn generation, but noted here since it isn't a fixed O(1) cost.

### Passion variance (if enabled)
1. Compute passion count as a smooth function of quality, same pattern as trait count, clamped the same way to the user-configured Passion count range (see the clamp-bounds clarification on Trait variance step 1 above).
2. **Clear all vanilla-assigned passions first** — the same clear-then-rebuild pattern Trait variance step 3 uses, and for the same reason: vanilla `GeneratePawn` already assigned passions before this postfix runs (Per-pawn flow step 1), and without an explicit clear, the weighted placement below would land *on top of* vanilla's own picks rather than replacing them, leaving the pawn with more passions than the quality-derived target and a final count uncorrelated with `quality` — silently defeating the "passion count should track quality" goal for every pawn, not just an edge case. There is no passion analog of Trait variance's forced-trait set (vanilla/quest content doesn't force specific passions the way it forces specific traits), so this clear is unconditional.
3. Build a weighted list of the pawn's skills using each skill's final absolute level (from the skill applier if enabled, vanilla level otherwise — chosen specifically so passion placement never needs a fallback branch for the skill-toggle-off case), **excluding any skill the pawn is totally disabled in** (work-tag/health-driven, `SkillRecord.TotallyDisabled`) — vanilla never assigns a passion to a skill a pawn can't use, and since this mod clears and fully rebuilds passions from scratch (step 2), nothing else would otherwise stop a disabled skill with a middling level from being sampled.
4. Weight each skill via softmax: `weight_i = exp((level_i - maxLevel) / temperature)`, where `maxLevel` is the highest level among this pawn's candidate skills and `temperature = lerp(minTemperatureFloor, maxTemperature, passionNoiseSlider)` with `minTemperatureFloor > 0` (same non-zero-floor rationale as trait `spread` above — a zero temperature would collapse to a hard "always pick the single best skill" cutoff). Subtracting `maxLevel` before dividing (the standard log-sum-exp stabilization) is load-bearing, not cosmetic: with levels up to 20 and `temperature` at its floor, the un-shifted form can overflow `exp()` to `Infinity`, and if two skills tie for the max, normalizing then divides `Infinity` by `Infinity`, producing `NaN` instead of a weight. Shifting by `maxLevel` guarantees the top skill's weight is exactly `1` and every other weight is a finite value in `(0, 1]`, with no change to the resulting (relative) distribution. Low temperature sharply favors the pawn's highest-level skills; high temperature approaches uniform weighting regardless of level.
5. Sample without replacement for placement; assign Minor/Major passion using vanilla's own ratio logic — **unverified, flagged for implementation-time confirmation**, the same caveat already given to `PawnKindDef.forcedTraits` and `GeneratePawn`'s call sites: this depends on whatever internal method/ratio vanilla uses to decide Minor-vs-Major, which may not be a stable public API and needs confirming against the target version's decompiled source before implementation, rather than assumed accessible.

### Growth-up variance (Biotech only, if `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` enabled)
Covers pawns that were Baby/Child-stage at generation and so were skipped by the early-exit in Per-pawn flow step 2 — including ones that arrived that way via a route other than natural birth (quest reward, adoption, wanderer join, purchase). `GrowthUpPatch` fires once, the moment such a pawn's `DevelopmentalStage` transitions to Adult:

0. Re-run the same gating checks Per-pawn flow step 2 applies at generation, using the pawn's *current* state rather than assuming it's unchanged since birth: skip if `applyToHostilePawns` is off and the pawn's current faction is hostile to the player (a captured/converted pawn's faction can change during childhood), skip if all three system toggles are off, and **skip unless `pawn.DevelopmentalStage == DevelopmentalStage.Adult` right now**. That last check is defensive, not redundant: the exact target method for this patch is explicitly unverified (see Architecture), and RimWorld's life-stage progression has more than one transition (Baby→Child, Child→Adult) — if the actual hook turns out to be a generic "developmental stage changed" callback rather than one specific to Child→Adult, this check is what stops the patch from rolling a full quality/variance application on a pawn who just became Child, not Adult, which would repeat exactly the mistake Per-pawn flow step 2 exists to prevent. `GrowthUpPatch` is gated by `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` independently of the three system toggles (see Architecture), but it must still honor `applyToHostilePawns`, the toggles themselves, and this stage check once it does run — nothing in "independent of the three system toggles" was meant to mean "independent of every other gate," it only means growth-up can be switched off as a category without touching the per-system toggles.
1. Roll this pawn's one-and-only `quality` value via `QualityRoller`, exactly as step 3 of the normal Per-pawn flow would have at generation.
2. Skill variance (if enabled): apply the same additive baseline-shift + noise as the normal flow, on top of whatever level the pawn has actually accumulated through childhood. **Known consideration, not a bug**: the skill shift range's defaults are one global setting shared across both paths, but a pawn who grew up in-colony reaches Adult with a childhood-accumulated skill distribution that isn't necessarily like a freshly-generated adult's vanilla distribution (it depends on how much they trained, which passions they picked up, etc.) — the same shift constants are applied regardless. This is accepted for v1 in the interest of a single shared setting rather than a second grow-up-specific shift range (YAGNI, consistent with how few settings this mod otherwise exposes); revisit only if playtesting shows grown-up pawns reading as noticeably different from directly-generated adults at the same quality.
3. Trait variance (if enabled): **never remove a trait the pawn already has** — growth-moment traits were deliberate choices made during play (often by the player), not vanilla-random filler, and are treated the same as the forced-trait set from Trait variance step 2. Re-derive the forced/disallowed sets *fresh*, from the pawn's *current* ideoligion/genes/kind def, exactly as Trait variance step 2 does at generation — do not reuse anything captured back at generation time, since years of in-game childhood can pass between generation and this transition, during which ideoligion conversion, gene-editing, or other changes could alter what's forced or disallowed. **One deliberate narrowing versus generation time**: this re-derivation only covers `PawnKindDef.forcedTraits` and gene-forced traits, not `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits` — that request object was specific to the pawn's original generation call and doesn't persist to be re-queried years later at growth-up. This is harmless *if* the quest-forced trait it would have named was already added back at generation time — but that premise itself isn't guaranteed for a pawn that was Baby/Child-stage at generation: Per-pawn flow step 2 early-exits the entire postfix (before `TraitVarianceApplier` ever runs) for any pawn that's Biotech-active and not yet Adult, and the same step notes vanilla itself "deliberately withholds personality traits... from Baby/Child-stage pawns." Whether vanilla's own `GeneratePawn` still honors `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits` for a still-developing pawn despite that general withholding is unconfirmed — **flagged for implementation-time verification**, same as the other vanilla-behavior assumptions in this spec. If vanilla does *not* grant it at that stage, the quest-forced trait was never placed to begin with, and since growth-up doesn't re-derive from this source either, it's permanently lost with no path to reintroduce it — the opposite of harmless. This only matters for the Baby/Child-at-generation path; a pawn already Adult at generation is unaffected, since `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits` handling there is standard vanilla behavior with no early-exit involved. **Add any currently-forced trait the pawn doesn't already have, unconditionally, regardless of the target count** — mirroring generation-time step 3's "re-add the forced set unconditionally" exactly, and independently of the top-up logic below, so a trait that only became forced *after* generation (e.g. a gene added mid-childhood, or a new kind-def requirement) is never silently skipped just because the pawn already met their quality-derived count via unrelated growth-moment traits. Unlike generation time, this pawn wasn't just cleared down to nothing first — it may already be holding growth-moment traits a newly-forced trait genuinely conflicts with (vanilla growth moments and a mid-childhood gene/kind-def change aren't guaranteed compatible with each other the way generation-time's forced set is guaranteed compatible with itself). In that specific case, the forced trait wins: remove the conflicting existing (non-forced) trait to make room and add the forced one, logging via `Log.Message` (rare — a forced-trait requirement introduced mid-childhood conflicting with an already-chosen growth-moment trait — but a real event, not an error). This is the one deliberate, narrowly-scoped exception to "never remove a trait the pawn already has" in this step: it only fires when a trait's *forced* status demands it, never as part of the quality-driven top-up logic below, which still never removes anything. **Forced-vs-forced conflicts are a separate, harder case**: generation-time's exemption from conflict-checking between forced traits (Trait variance step 3) is only safe because vanilla builds that forced set once, atomically, and guarantees its own internal consistency. Growth-up's fresh re-derivation can pull together forced traits from sources that were never designed to coexist — e.g. the pawn's original kind-def still forces trait Y while a gene added mid-childhood now also forces a conflicting trait X — and nothing in vanilla guarantees *that* combination is compatible. If two currently-forced traits genuinely conflict with each other, add them in a fixed priority order (kind-def-forced traits first, then gene-forced traits, matching the order they'd typically be introduced in a pawn's life) and skip adding a later forced trait that conflicts with an earlier one **already added this pass or already present on the pawn from generation time** — the "already added this pass" bookkeeping must be seeded with whatever forced traits the pawn already holds, not started empty — for this pawn population specifically (Baby/Child-stage at generation, and therefore skipped entirely by Per-pawn flow step 2's early-exit, meaning `TraitVarianceApplier`/Trait variance step 3 never ran for them), any such pre-existing forced trait got there from **vanilla's own kind-def/gene machinery independently of this mod**, not from this mod's Trait variance step 3, which only applies to Adult-at-generation pawns, or a newly-introduced gene-forced trait could silently displace a pre-existing kind-def-forced one instead of yielding to it despite kind-def's stated priority. Logging via `Log.Error` rather than `Log.Message` — this specific case reflects a genuine authoring-level contradiction between two content sources (not routine gameplay variance), so it deserves louder visibility than the routine eviction case above. **Forced-vs-disallowed is the same class of contradiction and gets the same resolution**: since the forced and disallowed sets are re-derived fresh from independent sources (e.g. a gene grants a forced trait at birth, then a mid-childhood ideoligion conversion makes that same trait disallowed under the new precepts), a trait can end up in both sets simultaneously at growth-up in a way generation-time's atomically-built forced set never has to handle. When this happens, forced wins — add the trait unconditionally per the rule above — and log via `Log.Error`, for the identical reason: this is a contradiction between two content sources (a gene's requirement vs. an ideoligion's prohibition), not something this mod's variance logic can resolve on its own, so it should surface loudly rather than silently pick a side. Then compute the quality-derived target count; if the pawn already has that many (or more) traits (forced ones included), do nothing further (see the accepted limitation below). If under the target, fill only the remaining slots via the normal weighted-sampling procedure, excluding the current disallowed set and respecting conflicts against traits already present. **Accepted, not fixed**: the freshly-derived disallowed set is only ever used to filter *new* candidates — a trait the pawn already holds (e.g. from an earlier growth moment) that has since *become* disallowed (most plausibly via an ideoligion conversion) is never retroactively removed; the only removal path in this step is the forced-trait-conflict eviction above, which is a distinct trigger. A pawn can end up permanently holding a trait their current ideoligion forbids. This is consistent with, and follows the same "never remove growth-moment content" reasoning as, the rest of this step — called out explicitly here (rather than left implicit) since every other tension of this kind in this spec is flagged the same way.
4. Passion variance (if enabled): same preserve-don't-remove principle — compute the quality-derived target passion count, keep existing growth-moment passions as-is, and only add more (via the normal softmax weighting over current skill levels, sampling without replacement over skills that **don't already carry a passion and aren't totally disabled** — same exclusions as Passion variance step 3) if under target.
5. Wrapped in the same try/catch + `Log.ErrorOnce` fail-safe pattern as the main postfix — but the guarantee it delivers is weaker here, and worth being explicit about rather than implying parity with generation time. The main postfix's "fail-safe to vanilla" claim (Per-pawn flow step 5) works because it mutates an in-progress, not-yet-returned `GeneratePawn` candidate: a mid-way exception just means the caller gets an untouched (or discarded) result. `GrowthUpPatch` instead mutates an already-live, already-`Scribe`-saved pawn's fields incrementally in place across steps 2-4. An exception partway through (mid-loop in step 2, or after step 3's forced-trait eviction but before step 4 runs) leaves that persisted pawn in a partially-varied, inconsistent state — e.g. a growth-moment trait evicted with no forced replacement actually added, or a skill shift applied with trait/passion steps never reached — with no equivalent of "discard the candidate" available for a pawn that already exists in the save. **Mitigation, not a full fix — and narrower than a single deferred-apply block, because of a real data dependency**: step 4 (passion) needs step 2's and step 3's *actually-applied* results — the pawn's final skill levels and which skills are `SkillRecord.TotallyDisabled` after trait changes — read via vanilla methods off the live pawn, exactly the same dependency that forces trait-then-skill-then-passion ordering in the main Per-pawn flow (step 4). Deferring *all* of steps 2-4's mutations to one single final block, as a naive read of "compute first, apply last" might suggest, would mean step 4 plans its passion placement against a live pawn that hasn't actually received the step 2/3 changes yet — silently reintroducing the pre-fix ordering bug this spec's Per-pawn flow section exists to prevent, just at growth-up instead of generation. So the mitigation is scoped *within* each step, not across all three: for steps 2 and 3, compute that step's planned changes into local variables, then apply them to the live pawn immediately (before moving to the next step), narrowing the exception window to each step's own apply call rather than eliminating cross-step risk entirely; step 4 then correctly observes steps 2-3's real, already-applied effects when it runs. If an exception fires during any step's apply, log via `Log.Error` (not `Log.ErrorOnce`) — a live pawn left mid-mutation is a distinct, per-pawn event worth full visibility every time it happens, not a deduplicated-by-exception-type systematic-bug signal the way the main postfix's failures are.

**Idempotency guard**: unlike a discarded `GeneratePawn` candidate, a pawn `GrowthUpPatch` touches is live and persisted — if the patched method were ever invoked more than once for the same pawn (e.g. it turns out to be a re-evaluated property check rather than a genuine one-shot transition event, or another mod re-triggers it), re-running this section would stack a second additive skill shift permanently onto the saved pawn, which is real, cumulative corruption rather than a harmlessly-discarded retry. Since Save-Game Safety forbids persisting an "already processed" flag on the pawn, guard with an in-memory-only set (e.g. a `HashSet<Thing.thingIDNumber>` or `ConditionalWeakTable<Pawn, object>`, never `Scribe`-serialized) tracking pawns already handled this session; skip if already present. This is deliberately *not* save-durable — on a fresh game load the set is empty again. **This reload window is an open risk, not a settled one** (see Edge Case 16 for the equally-hedged restatement of this same risk): if the transition event can genuinely re-fire once immediately after a load, a pawn who already grew up (and was correctly varied) before that load would, on the very next load, look to the in-memory guard exactly like a pawn seeing the event for the first time — the guard cannot tell "genuinely growing up right now" apart from "re-firing post-load for a pawn already handled last session," since nothing about prior handling is (or, per Save-Game Safety, can be) persisted. Whether this scenario is actually possible depends entirely on the target RimWorld version's transition-event semantics, which is unverified — same caveat as the other implementation-time verifications flagged in this spec, but this one is higher-stakes than most, since if it does occur it stacks a second permanent skill shift onto an already-varied live pawn (see the opening of this paragraph).

**Accepted limitation — trait count can end up decoupled from quality whenever pre-existing traits alone meet or exceed the target**: this applies at *both* generation and growth-up, for the same structural reason — Trait variance step 5 only fills "remaining slots after the forced traits are placed," so if the forced-trait set alone (from quest scripting, kind def, or genes) is already at or above the quality-derived target count, zero slots remain and the weighted-sampling step never runs at all, regardless of `quality`. At growth-up specifically, this is compounded by vanilla Biotech growth moments routinely granting 1-3+ traits/passions during childhood, entirely independently of this mod's `quality` roll (which doesn't exist yet at that point) — so a low-quality pawn who happened to accumulate several growth-moment traits/passions as a child, or whose forced set is simply large, can end up at or above their own quality-derived target with no variance-driven adjustment to bring their count in line with a low roll. This is accepted rather than fixed in both cases: forcibly removing a forced or player-chosen growth-moment trait/passion to "correct" the count would violate the preserve-don't-remove principle above (and, for forced traits, would break the quest/kind-def/gene system that requires them) for the sake of a statistical property. It's the same class of gap as the passion-placement scope limit already documented on `PassionVarianceApplier` — a real, intentional gap in correlation for pawns with a large pre-existing/forced trait set, not a bug to chase during testing.

If `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` is off, `GrowthUpPatch` doesn't run at all and a pawn skipped at generation for being Baby/Child-stage simply stays vanilla for the rest of that playthrough, even after reaching Adult — a deliberate option for users who want variance to only ever apply to pawns generated as adults.

### Tier bio label
Purely cosmetic — has zero influence on any mechanic above. `TierLabelPatch` postfixes the description-generation method to append `"\n\n<color=#RRGGBB>{TierName}</color>"` to a pawn's bio text, but only after confirming the pawn is currently eligible (see the eligibility re-check on `TierLabelPatch` in Architecture) — otherwise it appends nothing, so a pawn the mod never touched shows no tier label at all rather than a fabricated one. Default color mapping: Incompetent = red (`#B33A3A`), Standard = white/default (no color tag), Specialist = blue (`#3A7CB3`), Prodigy = gold (`#D4AF37`) — not user-configurable in v1 (YAGNI; colors can become settings later if requested).

**Storage note, consistent with the Save-Game Safety rule**: the original per-pawn `quality` roll is never stored anywhere (no new field on `Pawn`, no `ThingComp` — that would violate "zero custom save-file footprint"). Instead, `TierLabelPatch` computes a **display-only "effective quality" approximation live, every time the description is requested**, rather than recalling the original roll. Concretely: normalize the pawn's average current skill level against an **absolute-level range**, not the skill-shift range directly — the shift range (default -6 to +6) is a *delta* centered on 0, while a skill level is an *absolute* 0-20 value, so inverse-lerping the level straight against the shift range would saturate the component to 1.0 for nearly every pawn (real skill levels are almost never negative, and routinely exceed 6, which is already `lerp`'s upper bound). Instead, offset the shift range by a fixed internal constant `assumedVanillaSkillBaseline` (e.g. 5, a rough approximation of a typical vanilla skill level before any variance) to get an absolute-level range `[max(0, minShift + baseline), min(20, maxShift + baseline)]`, and inverse-lerp the pawn's average current skill level against *that* range, clamped to [0,1]. Both bounds are explicitly clamped to the physically-achievable skill range, not left as the raw sums: the lower bound floored at 0 (with the stated defaults — baseline 5, shift range -6/+6 — the raw `minShift + baseline` sum is -1, which would otherwise bias every pawn's normalized value upward by a fixed offset, e.g. a level-0 skill reading as ≈0.083 instead of 0.0), and symmetrically the upper bound capped at 20 (skill levels are clamped to [0,20] per Skill variance step 3, so any user-configured "Skill shift range" pushing `maxShift + baseline` above 20 — e.g. a max shift ≥ 15 with the default baseline — would otherwise mean a pawn genuinely capped at level 20 could never normalize to a full 1.0, systematically deflating the pawns who should read as clearest Prodigy; invisible at the default max shift of +6, but real for wider user-configured ranges). **Degenerate-range guard**: if the resulting range has zero width (`minShift == maxShift`, a valid, non-inverted "Skill shift range" setting, or one that collapses to zero width after the clamps above — e.g. a very negative `minShift` clamped to 0 alongside a `maxShift + baseline` that also clamps to the same value), the inverse-lerp's division is undefined; in that case skip the skill-level component and use the trait-desirability component alone as the effective-quality estimate (or, if trait variance is also disabled, fall through to imprecision #3's "toggle was off" handling below) rather than dividing by zero. Separately, normalize the pawn's average current trait desirability score against the same *clamped* bounds used by trait selection — `observedMinScore`/`observedMaxScore` (i.e. `meanScore ± zMultiplier·stdDevScore`, clamped to the actual observed min/max per Trait desirability scoring, not the raw unclamped formula) — (inverse-lerp, clamped to [0,1]), and take the **unweighted mean of those two normalized components** as the effective-quality estimate compared against the Tier display thresholds. Passion contributes no component to this estimate at all — there's no analogous "passion desirability scale" to normalize against the way skill levels and trait scores have one. Two consequences worth being explicit about, both acceptable in isolation: (1) nothing new is ever written to save data, by construction — the label is a pure read-time UI computation, not stored state; (2) the displayed tier reflects the pawn's *current* stats, so if a player later edits the pawn via dev tools or another mod changes their skills, the label updates accordingly rather than staying frozen to a historical roll — this is a reasonable, arguably more honest semantic ("this is how good this pawn currently is") rather than a bug.

**Known imprecisions — the "effective quality" readout is an inherently rough reconstruction, for several independent reasons**, all accepted as the cost of never storing the original roll (Save-Game Safety forbids it) rather than fixed:

1. **Clamp saturation biases extremes toward the middle**: the skill-level component is read back from levels that are clamped to [0, 20] (Skill variance step 3), so a pawn whose true shift would have pushed a skill past either clamp boundary loses exactly the signal that would have marked them as most clearly Prodigy or Incompetent. Concretely, at quality→1 with the default +6 max shift, a skill that started at a middling vanilla level of 16 becomes 16+6=22, clamped to 20 — 2 points of "true Prodigy" signal are gone; symmetrically at quality→0 with the default −6 min shift, a skill starting at 4 becomes 4−6=−2, clamped to 0. Both directions push the reconstructed estimate toward the center, most pronounced exactly at the settings Testing Plan item 5 already exercises.
2. **Vanilla backstory/pawn-kind variance confounds the skill-level component**: a pawn's final skill level is `vanilla_level + shift(quality) + noise`, and `vanilla_level` itself varies enormously by backstory, pawn kind, and age *before any variance is ever applied* — a "Genius"-style backstory pawn can sit many points above a generic colonist at the same `quality`. Since the original `vanilla_level` isn't stored either, there's no way to subtract it back out and isolate `shift(quality)` from the current level alone — two pawns with an identical `quality` roll but different backstories can read as different tiers, and in principle a low-quality pawn with an unusually skill-heavy backstory could still read as "Prodigy." This is a distinct, more pervasive confound than clamp saturation (#1): it affects every pawn's skill-level component, not just ones near an extreme.
3. **Either component — or, in the worst case, both simultaneously — can carry zero real signal, not just imprecise signal**: if `enableSkillVariance` was off when this pawn was processed, its skill levels are pure unmodified vanilla data, and the skill-level component is reading noise, not signal; the identical problem applies to the trait-desirability component whenever `enableTraitVariance` was off. `TierLabelPatch`'s eligibility gate (Architecture) only requires that *not all three* system toggles be off — it was never designed to check that the *specific* toggles feeding this two-component average were on. Concretely: a pawn processed with only passion variance enabled (skill and trait variance both off) still passes the eligibility gate, and gets a full color-coded label computed as the mean of two components that are *both* 100% vanilla noise in that configuration — not merely imprecise, but entirely unfounded. The approximation has no way to detect this after the fact, since (per Save-Game Safety) nothing about which systems were actually enabled for a given pawn's roll is ever stored.
4. **A pawn who grew up while `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` was off reads as if they'd been rolled, even though they were permanently left vanilla**: per Growth-up variance's closing paragraph, such a pawn stays 100% vanilla for the rest of that playthrough once they reach Adult. But by the time they're Adult, they pass every one of `TierLabelPatch`'s eligibility checks (humanlike, non-hostile, toggles on, `DevelopmentalStage == Adult`) — none of those checks can distinguish "grew up and got a real roll" from "grew up while growth-up variance was declined," since that fact isn't (and, per Save-Game Safety, can't be) stored anywhere. Unlike #1-3 above, this isn't an imprecision in the *value* shown — it's a case where a label appears at all for a pawn who should structurally never get one, and it's permanent (doesn't require any settings to have changed over the pawn's lifetime, unlike Edge Case 19's milder version of this same root problem).
5. **Changing "Skill shift range" mid-save retroactively rescales every already-generated pawn's displayed tier**, even though neither their actual stats nor their original `quality` roll changed. Unlike the auto-derived trait-score bounds (`observedMinScore`/`observedMaxScore`), which only rebuild on a mod-list-driven, startup-only cache refresh (Edge Case 10), the skill-shift range is a live user-editable slider (Settings Schema), and per the Storage note above, the skill-level component's absolute-level normalization range is derived directly from it. Widening or narrowing that range after pawns already exist shifts where their current (unchanged) average skill level lands on the [0,1] scale, so the same pawn can read as a different tier before and after a settings change with nothing about the pawn itself having happened in between.

All five imprecisions above share the same root cause and the same reason they're accepted rather than fixed: fixing any of them would require storing something about a pawn's actual variance history, which Save-Game Safety categorically forbids. The label is fundamentally a best-effort cosmetic guess, never a reliable readout, and nothing above changes that it never affects any mechanic.

**Implementation-time verification flagged**: the exact method that generates a pawn's bio-tab description text needs confirming against the target RimWorld version's decompiled source before implementation (naming it precisely here would be asserting unverified certainty, consistent with the similar caveat already flagged for `GeneratePawn`'s call sites above). This verification should also confirm the method is distinct from whatever generates the pawn's *inspect string* (the tooltip/bottom-bar text) — in standard RimWorld these are separate APIs (bio description vs. inspect string), so this is expected to be a non-issue, but Non-goals explicitly rules out any inspect-string patch, so implementation should confirm the two don't turn out to share a code path before patching.

## Settings Schema

Persisted via `PawnVarianceSettings.ExposeData()` in mod config (never in save files):

- **Average pawn quality** slider (0-1, default 0.5): the mean of the shared Beta-distributed quality roll. This controls the *population's average outcome*, not correlation strength — cross-system correlation comes from all three appliers consuming the same per-pawn `quality` roll (see Per-pawn flow), which happens regardless of where this slider is set. Correlation *strength* is instead controlled by the three noise sliders below (lower noise = tighter correlation to the shared roll), **except** passion *placement* specifically when skill variance is disabled — see the scope-limit note on `PassionVarianceApplier` in Architecture; no noise slider restores that correlation in that one configuration.
- **Skill noise** slider (0-1, default 0.35): maps to the per-skill triangular-noise `magnitude` via `lerp(minMagnitudeFloor, maxMagnitude, slider)`, same named-constant/nonzero-floor pattern as trait and passion noise. Nonzero by default so quality is a *bias*, never a deterministic outcome — this is what guarantees a high-quality pawn can still roll a mediocre skill.
- **Trait noise** slider (0-1, default 0.35): maps to the trait weighted-sampling `spread` via `lerp(minSpreadFloor, maxSpread, slider)`. `minSpreadFloor` and `maxSpread` are named constants with sane defaults (not separately user-exposed — see rationale below). Nonzero-floor by default so trait quality is never a deterministic function of `target`.
- **Passion noise** slider (0-1, default 0.35): maps to the passion-weighting `temperature` via `lerp(minTemperatureFloor, maxTemperature, slider)`, same named-constant/nonzero-floor pattern as trait noise.
- **Skill shift range** (min/max, default -6 to +6): what quality=0 and quality=1 map to as the skill baseline shift. User-configurable — this is the direct replacement for the old per-tier shift brackets, now expressed as the single global range the continuous curve spans. Symmetric by default so quality=0.5 gives vanilla parity (see Skill variance step 1 for why this default was chosen over an earlier asymmetric one).
- **Trait count range** (min/max, default 1 to 6): what quality=0 and quality=1 map to as trait count. The `smallRandomJitter` added on top (Core Algorithms) is a small fixed-magnitude named constant, not separately user-exposed — it only smooths the count roll's rounding, it isn't a tunable "feel" knob the way the noise sliders are.
- **Passion count range** (min/max, default matching vanilla's typical 0-3): what quality=0 and quality=1 map to as passion count. Same jitter-constant note as trait count.
- **Trait target score range**: *not* a user-editable setting — auto-derived at startup as `meanScore ± zMultiplier·stdDevScore` (clamped to the actual observed min/max) across all currently-loaded `TraitDef`s (via `TraitDesirabilityCache`; see Core Algorithms > Trait desirability scoring for why a distribution-based bound rather than true min/max or an order-statistic percentile). This keeps quality=0/1 meaningfully calibrated to whatever trait mods are actually loaded — and stays well-behaved for small trait pools too — rather than a fixed constant that could be miscalibrated for an unusually mild or extreme modded trait pool. Directly serves the "works safely with any trait mod" goal.
- The Beta distribution's population-spread parameter (how much pawns vary in underlying quality), and the `minMagnitudeFloor`/`maxMagnitude`/`minSpreadFloor`/`maxSpread`/`minTemperatureFloor`/`maxTemperature`/`smallRandomJitter`/`ε` (quality-clamp epsilon, Quality roll)/`zMultiplier` (trait-score bound width, Trait desirability scoring)/`assumedVanillaSkillBaseline` (Tier bio label)/skill-offset reference magnitude (Trait desirability scoring category 1, e.g. ±6)/stat-offset-factor reference magnitude (Trait desirability scoring category 2, e.g. ±1.0)/social-effects reference magnitude (Trait desirability scoring category 4, e.g. ±20) constants above, are **fixed internal constants, not user-exposed** — deliberately deferred (YAGNI) since the settings above already cover the tuning power most users will reach for; exposing any of them as additional sliders is a plausible future addition if requested.
- `enableTraitVariance`, `enableSkillVariance`, `enablePassionVariance` — default on.
- `applyToHostilePawns` — default on.
- `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` (Biotech only; hidden/no-op if Biotech isn't active) — default on. Governs whether `GrowthUpPatch` runs at all (see Core Algorithms > Growth-up variance). Independent of the three system toggles: a user can want variance for adult-generated pawns while still preferring that any pawn who started life as Baby/Child in their playthrough simply stays vanilla once grown, rather than picking up a delayed quality roll.
- Tier display thresholds (effective-quality-approximation cutoffs for "reads as Incompetent/Standard/Specialist/Prodigy") — cosmetic only, never affect mechanics. Used in two places: (1) a live readout inside the Mod Settings window itself (e.g. "Average pawn currently reads as: Specialist" next to the average-quality slider), and (2) the color-coded tier label `TierLabelPatch` appends to a pawn's bio text (see Core Algorithms > Tier bio label). Both are covered by the Non-goals carve-out — the live readout lives inside the standard Mod Settings window itself, and the bio label is the one named exception beyond it. **Known imprecision, shared threshold set applied to two different quantities**: the settings-window readout (1) is computed directly from the Beta distribution's true mean `m` — an unbiased, exact value, since there's no real pawn to sample from. The bio label (2) is computed from `TierLabelPatch`'s "effective quality" reconstruction, which (per the five Known imprecisions on Tier bio label) is systematically compressed toward the middle by clamp saturation, confounded by backstory variance, and sometimes carries no real signal at all. Applying the same numeric cutoffs to both means the settings-window preview can disagree with what players actually see stamped on real pawns at the same slider position — e.g. the readout might say "Specialist" while most in-game pawns at that setting still display "Standard" due to reconstruction compression. Accepted for the same reason the five imprecisions themselves are accepted: a separate, differently-calibrated threshold set for each use would need its own justification and tuning pass, and the mismatch only affects a cosmetic preview label, never any mechanic.

## Edge Cases

1. Average quality slider or any noise slider set to an out-of-range value (e.g. hand-edited config XML) → clamped to [0,1] on load rather than throwing.
2. Trait/passion sampler runs out of eligible candidates → under-fill gracefully, log once at message verbosity.
3. All three toggles off → early-exit before the quality roll even happens; pawn is 100% vanilla.
4. Non-humanlike pawns → always skipped, unconditionally.
5. Hostile-pawn check uses `pawn.Faction?.HostileTo(Faction.OfPlayer)`, null-guarded (faction-less pawns default to "apply").
6. Mod list changes between saves (trait mod added/removed) → desirability cache and trait weight tables are rebuilt fresh at each startup from currently-loaded Defs; nothing stale persists.
7. Harmony patch throws for any reason → caught, logged via `Log.ErrorOnce` keyed by exception type+stack (so a recurring systematic bug keeps surfacing rather than going silent after the first pawn), vanilla pawn stands untouched. `verboseLogging` dev setting rethrows instead, for manual testing.
8. Noise slider at 0 for a system → its `spread`/`temperature` sits at the nonzero floor constant, not exactly 0 (see Core Algorithms) — output is close to deterministic-by-quality but never a hard cutoff.
9. A min/max range setting (skill shift, trait count, passion count) is hand-edited so min > max → swapped on load rather than silently inverting the quality→outcome relationship. The four Tier display thresholds (Incompetent/Standard/Specialist/Prodigy cutoffs) get no analogous swap/clamp guarantee if hand-edited out of ascending order — low-stakes, since they're cosmetic-only and never affect mechanics (unlike the min/max ranges above, which drive actual variance outcomes), but worth noting they're not covered by this same protection.
10. Settings change detection: `PawnVarianceSettings` marks the Beta `α`/`β` cache dirty whenever `Write()` fires from the Mod Settings window (RimWorld's settings UI updates live, with no explicit "Apply" step) — the cache is lazily rebuilt on next access after being marked dirty, not on a fixed schedule, so slider changes take effect without requiring a restart. This does **not** include the trait score bounds (`observedMinScore`/`observedMaxScore`) — per Trait desirability scoring and Trait variance step 5, those depend only on which `TraitDef`s are currently loaded and the fixed internal `zMultiplier` constant, never on any user-facing slider, so nothing a user changes via `Write()` should invalidate them; they're rebuilt only when the loaded mod list changes (i.e. effectively only at startup, since RimWorld requires a restart to change mods).
11. Biotech Baby/Child-stage pawn that reaches the postfix (regardless of arrival route — birth, quest reward, adoption, wanderer join, purchase; see the still-open question in Per-pawn flow step 1 about whether every route actually reaches it) → skipped; no trait/skill/passion variance applied at that point, vanilla's own progressive growth-moment system is left untouched.
12. Same pawn later transitions to Adult → if `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` is on *and* the pawn currently passes the hostile-pawn/toggle gating (step 0 of Growth-up variance), `GrowthUpPatch` rolls quality and applies variance once, preserving (never removing) any traits/passions already granted via growth moments and only topping up to the quality-derived target count. If `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` is off, or the pawn fails the gating (e.g. still hostile and `applyToHostilePawns` is off), the pawn stays vanilla at that transition.
13. A pawn is already Adult-stage at generation (e.g. an adult quest reward or purchased slave) → normal Per-pawn flow applies at generation as usual; `GrowthUpPatch` never fires for it, since it never crosses a Child→Adult transition.
14. Biotech not installed → `DevelopmentalStage` check and `GrowthUpPatch` are no-ops (all pawns are effectively "Adult"); behavior is identical to the pre-Biotech-aware spec.
15. A Child-stage pawn's ideoligion changes (e.g. conversion) before they reach Adult → `GrowthUpPatch` re-derives the disallowed-trait set from the pawn's *current* ideoligion at the moment of transition, not whatever was true at generation, so a top-up never hands out a trait the pawn's present-day ideoligion forbids.
16. The Child→Adult transition method somehow fires twice for the same pawn *within one session* (implementation detail flagged for verification — see Architecture) → the in-memory processed-pawn set causes the second firing to no-op. Re-firing *across* a save/reload is a separate, still-open risk, not a solved one: the in-memory set is empty again after reload, so if the transition event can genuinely re-fire immediately post-load for a pawn already varied in a prior session, the guard cannot distinguish that from a genuine first firing and a second skill shift would stack permanently — see the Idempotency guard paragraph above for the full hedge; this needs verifying against the target version's actual transition-event semantics before implementation, not assumed safe.
17. A pawn accumulates enough growth-moment traits/passions during childhood to already meet or exceed their eventual quality-derived target → growth-up variance adds nothing further for that system (see the accepted limitation in Growth-up variance); the pawn's final count may end up uncorrelated with their quality roll. Documented as intended, not a bug.
18. `TierLabelPatch` opens the bio tab for a pawn who was excluded from variance (hostile pawn with `applyToHostilePawns` off, all three toggles off, or a Biotech Baby/Child pawn) → no tier label is appended at all, since the patch re-checks the same eligibility gates as the main postfix against the pawn's current state before computing anything.
19. Settings are changed (e.g. `applyToHostilePawns` flipped) sometime after a pawn was generated → `TierLabelPatch`'s eligibility re-check uses *current* settings, not whatever was in effect when that specific pawn was generated, so it's possible (rare, and an accepted consequence of never storing "did this pawn actually get a roll") for the label's gating decision to disagree with what actually happened to that particular pawn at generation time. Cosmetic only, never affects mechanics.
20. A Biotech pawn grows up to Adult while `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` is off → per Growth-up variance, they stay 100% vanilla permanently, but `TierLabelPatch`'s eligibility checks (humanlike/hostile/toggles/`DevelopmentalStage`) can't detect this specific history and will still show a fabricated tier label once they're Adult — a permanent, structural version of the same "can't know a specific pawn's actual history" limitation as Edge Case 19 and imprecision #4 on the Tier bio label. Accepted, not fixed, for the same Save-Game-Safety reason.
21. An Adult pawn is generated hostile with `applyToHostilePawns` off (skipped, stays 100% vanilla per Per-pawn flow step 2), and is later **captured and recruited** into the player's faction (or otherwise converted) → unlike a Biotech Baby/Child pawn, there is no growth-up-style re-check for Adult pawns whose faction changes post-generation, so this pawn never gets a chance at a quality roll and stays vanilla for the rest of the save despite becoming a regular colonist. Accepted as an extension of the same "no retroactive re-roll" policy in Non-goals (which frames it around mod-install timing and Baby/Child eligibility, but the same rationale — this mod only rolls quality once, at the one specific moment a pawn is eligible, never retroactively — applies equally here). Not fixed via a `GrowthUpPatch`-style analog, since unlike the Child→Adult transition (a single well-defined Biotech life-stage event), "an Adult pawn's faction became the player's" has multiple, less clearly-scoped triggers (recruitment, ideoligion conversion, quest resolution, faction goodwill events) that would need their own patch point(s) and their own eligibility semantics — treated as a possible future addition rather than v1 scope.

## Save-Game Safety

Hard architectural rule: **the mod adds nothing to the save file** — no `GameComponent`, `WorldComponent`, `ThingComp`, or custom `Scribe`-saved types, and no new Defs. Settings live in mod config XML, not the save. Per-pawn effects are written through vanilla-typed fields (`pawn.story.traits`, `pawn.skills`, passion fields) that RimWorld already saves regardless of this mod's presence.

Consequences, documented as intended behavior:
- **Add mid-save**: only pawns generated after this point get variance; existing pawns are untouched (no retroactive rerolling).
- **Remove mid-save**: the Harmony patch stops applying; new pawns generate as pure vanilla; existing pawns' already-saved traits/skills/passions remain intact with no Scribe errors, since none of that data ever depended on this mod's assembly.

This rule constrains all future feature work on this mod: no persistent custom save-file state, ever.

## Testing Plan

Manual/in-game verification (no automated harness — impractical for a Harmony-patched game mod):

1. **Distribution smoke test**: generate ~30-50 pawns across starting pawns/wanderers/prisoners, confirm quality/skill/trait/passion distributions roughly track the configured average-quality slider, with no visible clustering/discontinuity around any particular quality value.
2. **Noise slider sweep**: for each of the 3 noise sliders independently, verify 0 produces an outcome tightly clustered around the quality-driven baseline — *not* a literally deterministic single value, since the noise floor is deliberately nonzero (Edge Case 8) — and higher values visibly loosen that system's correlation with the shared quality roll.
3. **Toggle matrix**: manually verify all 16 combinations of the 4 toggles (skill/trait/passion/`applyToHostilePawns`), especially passion placement when skill variance is off — confirm passion *count* still tracks the quality slider but *which* skills get passions looks uncorrelated with it, which is expected (see `PassionVarianceApplier`'s scope-limit note), not a bug to chase — and hostile-pawn generation when `applyToHostilePawns` is off (spawn a raid, confirm raiders stay vanilla).
4. **Mid-save add/remove + mod-list change**: save with mod active → remove mod → reload (confirm no Scribe errors, existing pawns intact) → re-add (confirm new pawns get variance again) → separately, save with a trait mod loaded, remove that trait mod, reload, generate new pawns (confirm desirability cache rebuilds cleanly against the smaller trait pool with no stale references).
5. **Extreme settings**: average quality at 0 and 1, all noise sliders at 0 and 1 — confirm no crashes or infinite sampling loops, and confirm (not flag as a bug) that pawn-to-pawn spread visibly narrows at the average-quality extremes — see the Quality roll note on fixed concentration.
6. **Compatibility smoke test**: run with a popular trait-expansion mod loaded; confirm modded traits enter the weighted pool with sane-looking desirability scores.
7. **Config tampering**: hand-edit the mod's config XML with out-of-range slider values and an inverted min/max range; confirm both are clamped/swapped on load rather than throwing.
8. **Non-humanlike check**: generate/spawn animals; confirm they're untouched regardless of settings.
9. **Tier bio label**: open several pawns' bio/character tab, confirm the color-coded label appears, matches roughly what their actual stats suggest, and never appears for non-humanlike pawns; edit a pawn's skills via dev tools and reopen the bio tab, confirm the label updates to reflect current stats rather than staying frozen. Separately, confirm no label at all appears for: a raider generated with `applyToHostilePawns` off, a pawn generated while all three system toggles were off, and (with Biotech active) a Baby/Child-stage pawn who hasn't grown up yet.
10. **Biotech developing pawns**: with Biotech active, generate/spawn Baby and Child-stage pawns (birth in colony, and separately a quest reward/adopted child) and confirm none of them get trait/skill/passion variance while still developing, and that growth moments proceed exactly as vanilla. Age one up to Adult with `applyVarianceOnGrowUp` on and confirm variance applies once at that transition without removing any trait/passion already granted by a growth moment. Repeat with the setting off and confirm the pawn stays vanilla after growing up **and note (don't treat as a bug) that the tier bio label still appears for them anyway — see Edge Case 20.** Separately: age up a hostile-faction Child with `applyToHostilePawns` off and confirm growth-up is skipped; if feasible, force the transition method to fire twice on the same pawn (dev tools / repeated save-reload at the boundary) and confirm no double-shift; convert a Child's ideoligion mid-childhood, then age them up, and confirm no top-up trait violates their current ideoligion; give a Child a growth-moment trait, then introduce a conflicting forced trait (e.g. via dev-mode gene editing) before they age up, and confirm the forced trait wins (old one removed, logged) rather than being silently skipped. Also test the two remaining growth-up conflict classes added since: give a Child two mutually-conflicting forced traits from different sources (e.g. a kind-def-forced trait and, mid-childhood, a gene-forced trait that conflicts with it) and confirm kind-def wins by priority order with a `Log.Error`, not just `Log.Message`; separately, grant a gene-forced trait, then convert the Child's ideoligion mid-childhood to one that disallows that same trait, age them up, and confirm the forced trait still gets added (forced wins over disallowed) with a `Log.Error`.
11. **Quest-reward and trader pawns**: generate pawns via a quest reward and via a trader-redress/exchange interaction; confirm they receive variance the same as any other humanlike pawn (per Goals bullet 5) — this is the single highest-stakes flagged assumption in the spec (Per-pawn flow step 1), so don't skip it. If they come out vanilla, that confirms the flagged fallback (a second patch point) is actually needed before shipping.
12. **Recruitment/conversion permanence (Edge Case 21)**: spawn a raid with `applyToHostilePawns` off, confirm raiders stay vanilla, then capture and recruit one; confirm they remain permanently vanilla after joining the colony rather than picking up a delayed quality roll.
13. **Tier label rescale on settings change (5th Tier bio label imprecision)**: note a pawn's displayed tier, then change the "Skill shift range" setting, reopen that same unchanged pawn's bio tab, and confirm the displayed tier can shift purely from the setting change — expected behavior, not a bug, but worth confirming it actually happens as described rather than staying frozen.
14. **Trait-before-passion ordering**: with both trait and passion variance enabled, find or force (dev tools) a pawn to roll a trait that disables a work tag their highest-level skill belongs to; confirm that skill is excluded from passion placement (per Passion variance step 3's totally-disabled exclusion) rather than still receiving a passion, which would indicate trait variance ran after passion variance.
15. **Quest-forced trait on a Baby/Child pawn, through growth-up**: obtain a quest-reward or adopted Baby/Child-stage pawn from a quest specifically known to force a particular trait (`PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits`), and check immediately at generation whether that trait is actually present — this empirically resolves the flagged-unconfirmed question in Growth-up variance step 3 about whether vanilla grants `PawnGenerationRequest.ForcedTraits` to still-developing pawns despite generally withholding personality traits from them. Then age the pawn up to Adult and confirm the trait is still present (this mod never removes it regardless of the answer, per "never remove a trait the pawn already has") — but if the trait was *never* present at generation in the first place, that's the "permanently lost, no path to reintroduce" scenario the spec flags, and confirms a fix (or an explicit accepted-limitation writeup) is needed before shipping rather than assumed harmless.
